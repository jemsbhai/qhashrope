# Use cases

Applications of the qhashrope pack-and-verify prototype, one folder each. Every case uses the same two primitives: superdense-coding packing (2 bits per transmitted qubit over shared Bell pairs) and quantum fingerprint verification (Ablayev-Vasiliev-style fingerprints compared with a SWAP test).

**Selection principle.** Every case in this folder reduces to the same core question: are these two records equal, where the record is large, sensitive, or both, and the comparison happens under a communication or disclosure constraint. That reduction is the admission test, and it cuts both ways. Where two parties can exchange a classical hash directly and trust a single comparer, a plain hash already solves the problem completely, and those settings are not claimed here as quantum wins; each case below states its own classical baseline boundary. What the quantum construction adds, and what these cases are selected for, is the constrained regime: two parties sending only a short fingerprint to a referee (simultaneous-message model, no shared randomness, where classical fingerprints need on the order of sqrt(n) bits against O(log n) qubits), with the equality bit as the only disclosure.

As of 2026-11-08 the fingerprint uses the fixed encoding (position-seeded block hashing, Golay (24,12,8) error-correcting expansion, one global decision threshold of 0.75), and a two-server variant (per-bit SWAP tests split across two non-colluding referees, Golay radius-3 decision recombination) is measured at root level for the noisy setting. Every folder carries a runnable demo and a `results.json` with measured values from that pipeline. See the "Encoding fix, 2026-11-08" and "Two-server noise fix, 2026-11-08" sections of the root RESULTS.md.

## Comparative summary

One line per case: the setting, the privacy or communication need that motivates the fingerprint form, and the demo status. All demos were measured on 2026-11-08 with the fixed encoding (noiseless MPS simulator, 4096 shots, global threshold 0.75) unless the line says otherwise.

1. **Genomic identity verification.** Clinic or biobank against a second site, over sequence records up to whole genomes (about 3.2 billion bases; raw run output on the order of 100 GB). Privacy need: the sequence itself never moves; equality only. Demo: 12/12 classical and 12/12 quantum on 96 bp segments across identical and 1, 3, and 10 substitution classes.
2. **Model and dataset integrity.** Artifact holder against certifier, over gigabyte-scale checkpoints and datasets. Privacy need: moderate; the binding constraint is transfer size plus binding the check to a governed approval. Demo: 3/3 on synthetic checkpoint records, including a 3-byte change read as differ.
3. **Replica equality.** Two replica holders and a referee, over multi-kilobyte to arbitrary-size records on constrained links. Privacy need: the communication bound itself is the point; this is the theory anchor case. Demo: 3/3 on a 3,420-byte record, one-byte changes at either end both detected.
4. **Record linkage.** Two institutions (for example hospitals) over person records. Privacy need: pooling is prohibited; equality is the only lawful disclosure. Demo: 3/3 on fictional person records, a one-character field change detected.
5. **Superdense packing.** Sender and receiver on an intermittent link with pre-shared entanglement. Privacy need: none; the constraint is bandwidth during a contact window. Demo: superdense message accuracy 1.00 at depolarizing p = 0, 0.01, and 0.05.
6. **Molecular identity.** Checking site against a compound registry, over canonical structure strings indexing libraries of over 100 million records. Privacy need: the library is licensed or controlled and may not be distributed to checkers. Demo: 5/5 classical and 5/5 quantum on SMILES pairs, including the two pairs the pre-fix encoding failed.
7. **Compound library deduplication.** Two library owners, each with a proprietary collection. Privacy need: the library is the trade secret; only the overlap may be learned. Demo: an 8 by 8 cross-library run recovering exactly the 4 planted duplicates out of 64 pairs, no extras, no misses.
8. **Quantum chemistry result verification.** Producing lab against a rerun site or reproducibility checker, over result records from kilobytes to tens of gigabytes. Privacy need: restricted or oversized artifacts checked by a referee who sees neither. Demo: 3/3 on result records, a third-decimal coordinate change detected.
9. **Reaction protocol verification.** Two labs about to compare experimental outcomes, over encoded protocol strings. Privacy need: low to moderate; the point is provenance of the procedure, checked before the experiment. Demo: 3/3 noiseless, plus the identical pair under p = 0.02 noise (monolithic fails at 0.7385; two-server per-bit decision correct at 0 decided-differ bits).
10. **Reference standard verification.** Analytical or forensic lab against the issuing standards library, over certified substance records. Privacy need: the library is licensed or controlled and is not distributed. Demo: 3/3 noiseless, plus the identical pair under p = 0.02 (monolithic 0.7576, on the passing side this run; two-server decision correct).
11. **Formulation identity.** Sponsor against contract manufacturer, over a formulation record. Privacy need: maximal; the composition is a trade secret in both directions and equality is the only permitted output. Demo: 3/3 noiseless, a one-point proportion change detected, plus the identical pair under p = 0.02 (monolithic 0.7502, essentially on threshold; two-server decision correct).
12. **Cell line authentication.** Research lab against a cell bank's reference STR profile. Privacy need: sample profiles and reference collections are not pooled; the match bit is the deliverable. Demo: 3/3 noiseless, a one-allele marker difference detected, plus the identical pair under p = 0.02 (monolithic 0.7634 this run; two-server decision correct). Exact equality is stricter than the field's usual 80 percent match criterion; see the folder.
13. **Reference build verification.** Two analysis groups, over reference build and annotation release records. Privacy need: none of substance; the point is catching version drift before results are compared. Demo: 3/3 noiseless, same assembly with a different annotation release detected, plus the identical pair under p = 0.02 (monolithic fails at 0.7236; two-server decision correct).
14. **Outbreak strain matching.** Surveillance labs and a coordinating side, over isolate strain records. Privacy need: sequence data and sampling metadata are not pooled across institutions or borders; a match answer is what the network needs. Demo: 3/3 noiseless, a single variant position difference detected, plus the identical pair under p = 0.02 (monolithic fails at 0.7102; two-server decision correct).

Across the six folders that ran the noisy identical pair (cases 9 to 14), the monolithic estimate at p = 0.02 straddled the threshold from run to run (0.7102 to 0.7634, three calls wrong and three right), while the two-server per-bit decision called all six correctly with 0 decided-differ bits. That contrast is the current state of the noise story: the single global circuit is a borderline instrument at p = 0.02, and the decision-level recombination is not.
